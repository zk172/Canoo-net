Inflection of pronouns

  
 
Most pronouns are inflected. Different pronouns can have very different inflection patterns, and some pronouns, e.g. etwas, nichts, man, are invariable. For this reason, the inflection tables are shown in the descriptions of the individual pronouns (see Pronouns, Classes).

General rules:

Pronouns used attributively (before a noun):

Like articles and adjectives, most pronouns that are used attributively agree in gender, number and case with the noun they precede:

Agreement between pronoun and noun:

Gender masculine dieser/welcher/mein Stoff
neuter dieses/welches/mein Kissen
feminine diese/welche/meine Einladung
Number singular dieser/welcher/mein Stoff
plural diese/welche/meine Stoffe
Case nominative dieser/welcher/mein Stoff
accusative ohne diesen/welchen/meinen Stoff
dative mit diesem/welchem/meinem Stoff
genitive statt dieses/welchen/meines Stoffes


Pronouns used as substitutes for nouns:

Pronouns that are used as substitutes for nouns have the same gender and number as the nouns they replace:

Gender masculine Der Mann – Er spricht.
neuter Das Kind – Es spricht.
feminine Die Frau – Sie spricht.
facts Es – Das erstaunt mich...
Number singular Das Kind – Es spricht.
plural Die Kinder – Sie sprechen.

The case is determined by the role of the pronoun in the sentence.


Personal, reflexive and possessive pronouns express also the grammatical person of the noun they replace:

Person 1st person ich, mich, mein; wir, uns, unser
2nd person du, dich, dein; ihr, euch, euer
3rd person er/es/sie, sich, sein/sein/ihr; sie, sich, ihr


Features:

The pages under the heading Features describe some characteristics of pronoun inflection concerning a particular sub-group of pronouns.
